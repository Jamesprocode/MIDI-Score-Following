# Yamaha lead-sheet follower recreation: progress report (2026-10-15)

**Goal.** A strict recreation of Maezawa (2023, ISMIR LBD), "Automatic accompaniment from a lead-sheet by jointly tracking at chord and downbeat level":
- a causal TCN that outputs beat and downbeat (bar) phase from a piano roll, conditioned on a target BPM;
- a score follower that multiplies harmony evidence by the network's downbeat probability.

**Approach.** Train on Lakh with the paper's augmentation only. Test on JazzSAMBA piano. Anything the paper leaves open is labelled "our choice".

## 1. Training data (Lakh MIDI, matched subset)

- **Funnel:** 45,129 matched files → 32,522 pass the cleaning rules → 23,330 after removing note-identical copies → 13,612 song groups.
- **Split by group:** 12,895 train, 717 validation.
- **Rules,** each backed by measured evidence:
  - an explicit time signature, with meter events on bar lines;
  - notes on the 16th/triplet grid;
  - all meters kept, with the beat taken as the denominator note;
  - JazzSAMBA titles excluded.
- **Augmentation, as in the paper:**
  - a random combination of tracks;
  - random tempo drift;
  - random note addition and deletion;
  - the BPM condition given 50% of the time.

## 2. Training the timing network

| Run | Setup | Time | Val loss | Beat F | Downbeat F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lakh_r1 | Oyama recipe at lr 1e-3 | stopped | 6.93 (chance) | – | – |
| lakh_r2 | Oyama recipe, lr 1e-4, one whole song per step | 13 h (107 epochs) | 4.01 | 0.770 | 0.580 |
| lakh_r3b | 16 two-minute excerpts per step, warm-up to 1e-3, cosine decay to 1e-5 | 1.7 h (100 epochs) | 3.95 | 0.778 | 0.585 |

- **lakh_r1 and the learning-rate test:** lr 1e-3 stayed at chance, with loss spikes. A test on 2,000 songs chose 1e-4.
- **lakh_r2 vs lakh_r3b:** the two configurations tie, both on validation and on real data (Section 4). lakh_r3b is the one used from here on.
- **Configs:** `yamaha_timing/configs/oyama.json` and `batch16_cosine.json`.
- **More training is unlikely to help.** Over lakh_r3b's last 20 epochs, loss changed by less than 1% and downbeat F stayed flat.

## 3. What the synthetic validation measures

- **Tempo drift is not what makes the task hard.** Scoring 200 validation songs with drift removed entirely only moves downbeat F from 0.53 to 0.56.
- **Our drift is larger than a live jazz band's.** Measured against JazzSAMBA's live takes:
  - median tempo range within 4 bars: 34% in our data vs 14% in SAMBA;
  - bar-to-bar change: 2.0% vs 1.2%.
- **Real piano timing is looser than the training data.**
  - On SAMBA's click takes, piano onsets near a beat sit a median 31 ms from the click (90th percentile 68 ms), with swung eighths.
  - Lakh is mostly quantised.

## 4. Real performances (timing network alone)

Results with the BPM hint given (mir_eval F-measure at ±70 ms):

| | Beat F | Downbeat F |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic validation | 0.78 | 0.585 |
| JazzSAMBA async (click, 100 takes) | 0.51 | 0.30 |
| JazzSAMBA sync (live, 52 takes, repaired timing) | 0.54 | 0.37 |
| ASAP classical (1,067; oracle tempo) | 0.58 | 0.25 |

- **No full-take lock:** no SAMBA take reaches downbeat F 0.8; the median is 0.31. The network locks onto the beat for stretches, then drifts.
- **The BPM hint matters:** withholding it halves beat continuity (CMLt 0.27 → 0.15), which supports the paper's reason for having it.
- **Not explained by swing or genre:** swung and straight songs score the same (beat F 0.52 each), and so do all genres. Continuity drops at fast tempos: CMLt is 0.56 below 120 BPM and 0.22 above 170.
- **Interpretation:** the gap between pop training data and jazz playing is in timing feel (loose, syncopated comping), not in tempo drift.

## 5. Data problem found and fixed: JazzSAMBA sync piano MIDI

**The fault.**
- Sync piano MIDI is mistimed as distributed. The ticks follow a 175 BPM session clock, but each file is stamped with the song's written tempo.
- The MIDI also starts 1 to 21 s off the audio.
- As stored, onsets are spread evenly over the beat (phase concentration 0.04, against 0.36 on the click takes).

**The fix.**
- Rescale to 175 BPM.
- Fit each take's start shift by cross-correlating MIDI onsets with the take's own piano audio, without using any annotations.
- Validation: on correctly aligned click takes, the same method finds a shift of 0.
- After the fix, onset concentration is 0.32.
- Tool: `yamaha_timing/samba_sync_timing.py`.

**Next step:** worth reporting to the dataset authors.

## 6. Real-time feasibility

The network is streamed causally, one 50 ms frame at a time. Per-frame time, with a budget of 50 ms:

| Hardware | Re-run the 6.4 s window (reference) | Cached, one step per layer |
|---|---|---|
| Mac CPU (4 threads) | 10.8 ms | 1.8 ms |
| Mac GPU (MPS) | not supported | 2.6 ms |
| PACE CPU (8 threads) | 7.3 ms | 1.9 ms |
| PACE H100 | 4.8 ms | 1.0 ms |

- **Cached tracker:** it gives the same outputs as the reference; a test checks this.
- **Follower update:** 0.3–1.1 ms per update for Stage A, and about 10 ms per 50 ms update for Stage B.
- **Conclusion:** the whole system runs in real time on a laptop CPU. No GPU is needed.

## 7. Integration into the score follower (JazzSAMBA, piano input)

**Setup.**
- The existing benchmark filter, unchanged: a grid of possible (position, tempo) states, harmony observation, known start, written tempo.
- The input is the recorded piano MIDI. The earlier benchmark used bass and horn transcriptions.
- **Metrics:**
  - bar accuracy: the share of 200 ms checks in the correct bar;
  - beat accuracy: the share of checks in the correct beat;
  - position error, converted to milliseconds.
- **Which takes count:** click takes are trivially followed by a fixed clock (bar accuracy 0.999), so the live sync takes are the real test.

### Stage A: replace the rule-based rhythm cue with the network's downbeat

Live takes (52):

| Follower | Bar acc. (mean / typical take) | Beat acc. | Takes ≥ 0.9 | Lost takes | Error when not lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed clock | 0.08 | 0.03 | – | – | – |
| Harmony only, 8 positions/bar | 0.68 / 0.73 | 0.38 | 1 | 4 | ±217 ms |
| + rule-based onset cue, 8/bar | 0.67 / 0.72 | 0.37 | 1 | 3 | ±223 ms |
| + network downbeat, 8/bar | 0.74 / 0.84 | 0.54 | 8 | 6 | ±114 ms |
| Harmony only, 32/bar | 0.68 / 0.74 | 0.38 | 0 | 3 | ±215 ms |
| + rule-based onset cue, 32/bar | 0.73 / 0.77 | 0.46 | 5 | 3 | ±141 ms |
| + network downbeat, 32/bar | 0.75 / 0.84 | 0.58 | 12 | 6 | ±73 ms |
| Network downbeat alone, 32/bar | 0.54 / 0.64 | 0.44 | 9 | 18 | ±84 ms |

- **The swap works.** The network clearly beats the rule-based cue: tighter timing (±73 vs ±215 ms) and twelve takes followed almost perfectly, where harmony alone manages none.
- **Why takes get lost.** All six lost takes were played 1.22–1.51× faster than the written tempo. The filter's tempo range stops at 1.3×, and the network is told the written tempo throughout.
- **Earlier benchmark, for reference** (bass/horn transcription input): harmony bar accuracy 0.52 (async) and 0.54 (sync). Piano input is a much better harmony source.

### Stage B: what the paper adds

- **Tempo feedback:** the tracked tempo, low-pass filtered, is fed back as the network's BPM condition.
- **Tempo as a random walk drawn at each beat** (an HHSMM), constant within a beat.
- **Updates every 50 ms.**
- **Our choices where the paper is silent:**
  - tempo range 0.5–2× the start tempo, in 2.5% steps;
  - a 3% random-walk step per beat;
  - a 2 s low-pass time constant.

Same 200 ms checks as Stage A. Live takes (52):

| Follower | Bar acc. (mean / typical) | Beat acc. | Takes ≥ 0.9 | Lost takes | Error when not lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage A best (+ network downbeat, 32/bar) | 0.75 / 0.84 | 0.58 | 12 | 6 | ±73 ms |
| B1: 0.5–2× tempo range, fixed condition, 50 ms | 0.73 / 0.84 | 0.58 | 15 | 8 | ±76 ms |
| **B2: B1 + tempo feedback** | **0.77 / 0.85** | **0.61** | **15** | **5** | ±77 ms |
| B3: per-beat tempo walk + feedback (the paper's description) | 0.68 / 0.78 | 0.54 | 12 | 10 | ±74 ms |
| B4: B3 without the network (harmony only) | 0.67 / 0.70 | 0.40 | 0 | 2 | ±218 ms |
| B5: B3 started from the played tempo (oracle) | 0.80 / 0.83 | 0.63 | 15 | 3 | ±71 ms |

On the click takes (100), B1 and B2 reach 0.875 bar accuracy and lose no take. B3 drops to 0.72 and loses 14.

**What this shows.**
- **Tempo feedback helps** (B1 → B2): +5 points of bar accuracy and three fewer lost takes. With the written tempo, B2 is the best follower so far.
- **The network makes following precise.** Without it (B4), no take reaches 0.9 and timing error is ±218 ms. With it, error is about ±75 ms.
- **A correct target tempo matters.** The paper assumes the player supplies the target tempo. Starting from the played tempo (B5) gives the best mean, 0.80. SAMBA's written tempos are often far from what was played.
- **The paper's sentence alone is not enough.** "A tempo at each beat as a random walk", implemented literally with nothing pulling toward the target (B3), lets the follower lock onto half the real tempo. Example: On Green Dolphin Street is played at about 187 BPM and the follower settles near 95 BPM. This is the same octave error the network's BPM condition guards against, but here it happens inside the follower. The paper's HHSMM (Maezawa & Yamamoto 2017) presumably constrains this; the 2023 paper does not say how.
- **B6/B7 (running).** They add a weak per-beat pull toward the target tempo, fixed in advance at 0.02 (about 15% spread) rather than tuned on SAMBA. B6 uses the written tempo; B7 the played tempo.
- **Compute.** Stage B costs about 10 ms per 50 ms update on the Mac CPU, and 15–50 ms on PACE with 32 jobs sharing a node.

## 9. Open questions and next steps

1. **Follower tempo model (needed for a faithful baseline).** The paper's HHSMM details are in Maezawa & Yamamoto (2017, CHI), which we don't have. Our literal reading loses takes to octave errors. Options:
   - a weak pull toward the target tempo (B6/B7, running);
   - a bounded tempo range;
   - obtaining the 2017 paper.
2. **Target tempo.** The paper assumes the player supplies it. SAMBA's written tempos differ from what was played by up to 1.5×. Evaluating with both written and played tempo keeps this visible.
3. **Gap between pop and jazz.** On real jazz piano, the network alone falls from 0.78/0.585 to about 0.5/0.3 (beat/downbeat F). The likely cause is timing feel, not tempo drift. This argues for jazz-specific training data, such as SAMBA's train split, but only after the strict replication.
4. **Adaptive cue weighting.** Harmony and the downbeat fail in different places: harmony loses fewer takes but is imprecise; the network is precise but loses some takes. That supports weighting each cue by its reliability (the thesis direction).
5. **Data quality.** Report the SAMBA sync timing fault to the authors. Check note completeness and drift along each take.
6. **Notes.** Details and tests for each idea are in `yamaha_timing/RESEARCH_NOTES.md`.
