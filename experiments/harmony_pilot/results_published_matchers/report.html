Yamaha and Pardo: harmonic-scoring comparison

These are documented harmonic-component reconstructions/adaptations, not published full-system accuracy. No Yamaha trained downbeat model, HHSMM, accompaniment, or Pardo sequence follower has been run. Three async songs, bass + saxophone + trumpet MIDI; piano and drums are absent. No model was trained.

What was implemented

Yamaha / Maezawa (2023), Section 2.2: the harmonic log likelihood is the dot product of the current 88-note piano roll and a chord template, plus a written-melody term. We use the dot product directly, which preserves the exponential likelihood's ranking. Melody is unavailable and omitted. The paper does not specify numerical templates: binary chord membership and binary held notes are explicit reconstruction choices. The soft-template variant checks sensitivity to that missing detail, using the same heuristic as the previous pilot. We evaluate every 100 ms; the paper's timing-network input is 20 FPS.

Pardo and Birmingham (2002), CHORD segment labeling: reward matching pitch classes, subtract weights of extra notes, and subtract one for each missing template pitch class. Note weights count how many minimal note-on/off segments they span, not seconds. The root-weight tie rule is reported separately. We adapt segment selection to past-only sliding windows. The shared chart vocabulary adds chord qualities absent from the original six families while restricting candidates to this song's chart labels. Global/offline segmentation and the distinct lead-sheet follower are not reproduced.

Why the vocabulary adaptation matters

CHORD's original six chord families do not cover many written extensions or major/minor sevenths in these jazz charts. The table shows exact support under the original vocabulary. We retain the complete chart vocabulary for every main comparison rather than silently discard unsupported frames or simplify only one method's target.

Interpretation limits

  • All three songs are asynchronous fixed-tempo overdubs; this does not test natural ensemble tempo adaptation.
  • Scores concern the chart chord, not a unique bar occurrence or full position following. Repeated chords remain ambiguous.
  • These are transcription-derived bass/horn inputs; the Yamaha demonstration used piano. Results should not be attributed to the published system's performance.
  • Annotation timing is used only for scoring. Original MIDI time origins are retained, with no fitted time shift. No independent audio-versus-label verification was performed.
  • Preferred takes are primary. Alternate take results use the release's documented shared async annotation grid and are saved separately.
  • Three songs are an exploratory sample. No confidence calibration, learned weighting, streaming-latency claim, or held-out generalization result is established.
