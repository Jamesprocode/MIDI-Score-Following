# Synchronous timing provenance

Checked 2026-10-05. This is a documentation and metadata audit, not an independent audio-alignment verification. No benchmark timestamps, offsets, or source files were changed.

## Verified

- All **390 local sync FLAC files** are `*.debleeded.derived.flac` overlays. The audited local collection contains no original `mixture.flac` or original instrument `.flac` files. The unequal lengths therefore concern a **debleeded mixture versus debleeded stems**, not a demonstrated mismatch between original release assets. Non-mixture overlays have equal sample lengths within every take. Mixture overlays are longer in **38/52 take groups**, including **19/26 preferred groups**, by up to **5.504 seconds**. Evidence: `audio_headers.json` and `song_take_groups.json` in this directory.
- The official [demo README](https://github.com/pnlong/jazzsamba-demo/blob/main/README.md) identifies its full-length explorer audio as preferred-take stereo mixtures encoded to 24 kHz MP3. Its [explorer code](https://github.com/pnlong/jazzsamba-demo/blob/main/js/explorer.js#L1012) loads the catalog's annotation JSON and mixture URL together. Playback/annotation transport uses the audio element's `currentTime` directly (lines 98–110 and 982–986); no per-song timing-offset field or correction was found. Thus the public annotation display is intended to share the demo mixture timeline. This does not independently verify its alignment to the local MIDI.
- The [paper, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34931#S3) and [official recording description](https://github.com/pnlong/jazzsamba-demo/blob/main/about.html#L96) describe separately annotated synchronous takes, with bar timestamps and chart-derived chord/form annotations on a shared bar grid. They do not specify a MIDI/debleeding latency correction or explicitly certify sample-level alignment among all derived assets.
- The public [audio loader](https://github.com/pnlong/jazzsamba/blob/main/jazzsamba/audio.py#L22) reads file samples directly. Its stem-summing helpers preserve the first sample and pad shorter files at the **end** (lines 58 and 89–93). [Take](https://github.com/pnlong/jazzsamba/blob/main/jazzsamba/take.py#L129) and [Stem](https://github.com/pnlong/jazzsamba/blob/main/jazzsamba/stem.py#L25) distinguish original audio, optional debleeded audio, and transcription-derived MIDI paths; they expose no offset correction. This establishes loader behavior, not the export pipeline's behavior.
- For all 26 preferred sync songs, the last annotated bar ends before the local debleeded stems end, by **1.663–12.281 seconds**. Therefore the evaluated bar span is not simply extending past these audio files. Different total file lengths cannot identify a leading offset, time shift, or time scaling.

## Unresolved

The processing repository linked by the official repositories, `pnlong/jazz-standard-dataset`, returned 404 during this check. Its release README and AMT/debleeding export scripts could not be inspected; the Zenodo release README was not available locally. We therefore cannot establish from verified source code whether the exported MIDI and debleeded overlays preserve the original mixture's exact time origin, or whether any known transcription/export delay is present. The `.transcribed.derived.mid` filename alone does not settle this.

There is **no demonstrated systematic origin mismatch**, and the overlay-duration discrepancy alone is insufficient evidence for one. Nonetheless, synchronous accuracy remains provisional with respect to independent timestamp alignment. Favorable harmonic-following results are not alignment proof.

The benchmark preserves original MIDI seconds and annotation seconds, with no fitted shift or rescaling. Resolve the remaining uncertainty through the release export specification or an independent original-audio/MIDI content check at several locations; do not optimize an offset against chord or position accuracy.
