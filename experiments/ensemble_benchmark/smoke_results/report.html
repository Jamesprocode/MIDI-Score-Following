Every scorer chooses among the unique reduced chord labels in the known chart; this is chart-constrained recognition. Chord accuracy uses a single predicted label, including the root and the quality retained by the target scheme. Triad targets are primary; seventh targets are secondary. Pardo applies its root-weight tie rule; remaining ties use the fixed vocabulary order. Fractional-tie accuracy shares one point among remaining tied winners and is shown separately from hard classification accuracy. Input silence counts as an incorrect/abstaining chord prediction. Balanced accuracy is the per-song mean recall over represented target classes; root accuracy ignores quality. Coverage and tie rates help interpret sparse or ambiguous evidence.

Position tracking: shared follower and clock

Every harmonic method uses the same causal position/tempo filter and the same known unfolded chord plan, synchronized start position/time, and initial BPM. The constant-tempo clock uses that initialization without harmonic observations. Exact bar accuracy requires the correct unfolded bar occurrence, including its repeat/chorus. The within-two-beat rate is reported separately. Error columns are equal means over songs; the last column averages each song's median and is not a pooled median.

Async was recorded against a fixed timing plan. With the correct synchronized start and nominal tempo, a perfect or near-perfect clock is expected and does not demonstrate score-following ability. Sync is the more relevant condition for testing correction of tempo drift. Initial-tempo perturbations, when available below, test a distinct initialization stress condition.

Interpretation and verification

  • Maezawa-style scoring reconstructs the published harmonic dot-product form with declared template values; the published downbeat network, HHSMM and accompaniment system are not reproduced.
  • Pardo-style scoring adapts event-partition note weights and its matching/missing-tone score to causal windows and the benchmark vocabulary. Complete segmentation and lead-sheet sequence alignment are not reproduced.
  • The empirical profile is a small data-derived harmonic model, not a neural model. Its training split must exclude the target composition and all its takes; consult the saved configuration for the implemented split.
  • Repeated chord labels alone cannot identify a unique bar. Position results also depend on initialization, the shared filter, and the known unfolded form.
  • Transcription errors, held-note artifacts, anticipations, substitutions, and annotation disagreements can reduce chart compatibility. Valid MIDI syntax does not establish a correct transcription.
  • No adaptive cue-weighting model is tested merely by comparing these scorers. No result here establishes unseen-composition generalization for window or preprocessing selection.
  • Offline execution time is not streaming latency. This report does not claim real-time deployment or recovery from arbitrary repeats/skips.
  • The saved local audit found 524 MIDI files and 63 core files with predefined content-quality flags. MIDI meter tags do not reliably encode the chart meter; tempo metadata is used for tick-to-second conversion, not as proof of constant performed tempo.
